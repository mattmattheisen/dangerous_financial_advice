# Podcast Episode Framework

## Working title

**The Most Dangerous Financial Advice May Be the Advice That Looks the Smartest**

## Core setup

The episode begins with a synthetic retirement case that appears unusually thorough.

The household is detail-oriented.

The workflow uses multiple specialist agents.

The analysis contains assumptions, confidence scores, specialist outputs, and internal consistency.

One fact is missing:

**Does the pension have a COLA?**

## Narrative arc

### 1. The seduction of sophistication

Start with the premise:

People increasingly use LLMs to examine portfolios, retirement plans, taxes, and withdrawal strategies.

The risk is not necessarily an obviously stupid answer.

The more interesting risk is a polished answer that looks difficult to challenge.

### 2. The experiment

Explain the synthetic case.

The household wants to retire at 62.

The pension is known.

The COLA is not.

Nothing else is hidden from the model.

### 3. The multi-agent workflow

Walk through:

- intake
- retirement
- portfolio
- synthesis

Each specialist appears to add independent expertise.

### 4. The forensic reveal

Trace the COLA provenance backward.

Unknown.

Then assumed.

Then normalized.

Then inherited.

Then counted as agreement.

Then used to support confidence.

### 5. The skeptical control

Show what changes when one agent has a different objective:

Do not optimize the recommendation.

Try to break it.

The skeptical system asks:

> "Where did the COLA assumption come from?"

### 6. The broader lesson

The important distinction is not human versus machine.

It is:

**sophistication versus correctness**

and:

**detail density versus evidentiary density**

A system can become more elaborate without becoming more epistemically independent.

## Required disclosure

During the episode say clearly:

> "This was a small synthetic demonstration, not a statistical study of all AI systems."

If the pilot results are mixed, say so.

If the expected failure does not occur, that negative result becomes part of the episode.

## Candidate closing line

> "We did not test whether AI could make a stupid mistake. We tested whether it could make a sophisticated one."
